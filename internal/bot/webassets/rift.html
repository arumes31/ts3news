Your RPG build, tuned for action

Brawl brings your Abyss class, subclass, permanent stats and equipment into real-time combat. Large RPG stat values are compressed into a smaller action-game range, so Brawl HP, damage and armor differ from the numbers on your RPG character sheet. Stronger stats still help, but doubling an RPG stat does not double its Brawl value.

The current run keeps the build it started with. Changes to your character, gear or learned skills apply when you start a new expedition.

Armor, guard and barriers

Armor reduces incoming damage automatically. Guard then reduces remaining frontal damage by 82%; turn toward the attacker, because attacks from behind bypass guard. A barrier absorbs the damage left after those reductions until its protection runs out. Guarding is not immunity.

For example, if 10 damage remains after armor, a successful guard reduces it to 1.8. A barrier can absorb that remainder before it reaches your HP.

Depth and melee range

Moving up or down changes your depth on the battlefield. Jumping changes height instead. For a basic melee strike, face the enemy, close the horizontal gap and line up at roughly the same depth. An enemy just above or below you can look close enough but still be outside the strike's narrow depth range.

Try moving up or down to align your fighter's feet with the target, then attack. Spells have their own ranges; use the skill reference for each ability.

Low cover

Low cover blocks walking and basic melee strikes across its footprint. Circle it or jump over it to reach the other side. Friendly and enemy projectiles pass over low cover, so standing behind it does not protect you from ranged attacks. Area attacks use their own marked ranges.

Tall pillars block walking, jumping, melee strikes and projectiles from either side. Circle the pillar to get a clear shot.

Nonvisual combat support & limits

Rift Brawl is a fast-paced 60fps side-scrolling action brawler. While assistive engineering is built into the HUD, audio system, menus, and controls, real-time spatial combat has inherent nonvisual limitations that players should know.

Supported nonvisual & assistive features

  • Polite live region alerts: Room changes, boss attack telegraphs, health danger warnings, and tier transitions are announced to screen readers via #rift-announcer.
  • Accessible encounter summaries: After every room clear, defeat, or expedition end, a full structured battle breakdown is presented in #rift-last-encounter with keyboard focus shortcuts.
  • Directional audio & threat cues: Off-screen enemy projectiles and boss attack windups emit stereo-panned sound and optional directional visual captions (← / →).
  • Audio cues & soundscapes: Distinct cues play for low health, ready ultimate/finisher abilities, guard mitigation, and floor pickups. Channel volumes can be individually customized.
  • Semantic controls & screen reader labels: Action buttons have clear speech names (Attack, Jump, Guard) isolated from keycap icons (WCAG 2.5.3) and announce keyboard shortcuts.

Technical limits of real-time combat

  • Continuous spatial positioning: Rapid sub-second 2D coordinates of multiple moving monsters and projectiles are not streamed as speech or braille to avoid severe audio buffer overflow and cognitive overload.
  • Depth & melee alignment: Melee combat requires aligning vertical depth (Y axis) within tight pixel tolerances. Without sight, hitting moving agile targets requires area-of-effect spells or directional guessing.
  • Dynamic floor hazards: Moving hazard zones telegraph visually and through spatial audio, but exact perimeter boundaries lack real-time continuous haptic or screen reader fences.
  • Reaction timing: High-tier boss counters and parry windows demand 200–400ms reactions to visual flash telegraphs, which live screen reader engines cannot announce with sub-frame latency.

Recommended nonvisual strategies

  • Frequent tactical pauses: Press Esc / Menu anytime to freeze combat. The pause state maintains audio silence while allowing review of HUD status, controls, and active settings.
  • Defensive positioning & guard: Directional guard mitigates 82% of frontal incoming damage. Backing into a wall or corner limits flank angles and lets you face outward while holding guard.
  • Area-of-effect & tracking skills: Equip wide-cone abilities, barriers, and ranged builders that do not require exact pixel-level vertical depth alignment.
  • Dedicated practice drills: Use isolated practice modes (Movement, Jump, Guard) to learn audio timings and ability ranges with infinite HP and no progression risk.
